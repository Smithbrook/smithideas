Smith Ideas ships tiny, single‑purpose utilities built to scratch an itch.

Most of them start the same way: a small, specific problem (or "want") that doesn't need a team. Some come together in an hour. Others take a few quiet afternoons. None of them start with a roadmap, a funnel, or a growth target.

The assignment is simple: it has to work, and it has to feel good the hundredth time you use it.

It's easy to bloat a simple tool with accounts, dashboards, and settings nobody asked for. Most of what I build leaves that stuff out on purpose. A good utility does its job and gets out of your way. That's the bar, project after project.

The Check

Honestly, the part I like most isn't building the thing — it's naming it. There's a specific kind of fun in taking a rough idea and pressure-testing it: who's it actually for, is the problem real, does the name fit. I do this constantly, for my own projects and for fun, long before anything gets built.

The Check is that same habit, handed to you. Six questions, run against a fixed framework, the same for everyone — no sign-up, no AI, nothing stored. It won't tell you whether your idea will work. It'll tell you where the plan is thin, so you find that out in a minute instead of six months in.

I'm Ray. I grew up in Brooklyn, live in Los Angeles now, I'm a husband, dad, Christian, and I've spent a lot of time trading, investing, and tinkering with side projects that probably should've died sooner than they did.

Domains

One habit that comes with liking this stuff too much: I've picked up more domain names over the years than I've ever gotten around to building. If The Check tells you your idea has real legs, go make sure the name's actually available before you get too attached to one that isn't.
